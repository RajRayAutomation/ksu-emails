Lion's Mane and neurogenesis

For most of medical history, it was believed that the adult brain could not generate new neurons. Neurogenesis - the creation of new brain cells - was thought to stop in childhood.

That understanding has been comprehensively revised. The adult brain does produce new neurons, particularly in the hippocampus - the region associated with memory and learning. And certain compounds significantly upregulate this process.

Lion's Mane mushroom contains hericenones and erinacines - compounds that stimulate the production of Nerve Growth Factor (NGF) and Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF). Both are critical for neuronal survival, growth and the formation of new synaptic connections.
