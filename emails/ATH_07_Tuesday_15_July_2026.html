Recovery in summer heat - why it takes longer than you think

Heat stress, cortisol, sleep quality. The three factors slowing your recovery right now

Dear [firstname,fallback=there],

You trained hard. You ate well. You slept eight hours. But you woke up still heavy, still tired. Sound familiar? In summer, recovery operates on a different timeline.

The summer recovery gap

Three things compound in summer to slow recovery. First, heat stress during training creates more cellular damage than the same session in cooler conditions. Your body has to repair more.

Second, elevated cortisol from heat exposure impairs the anabolic signalling that drives muscle repair. The hormonal environment post-training is less favourable.

Third, heat disrupts sleep quality. Even a 1 degree rise in core body temperature at bedtime meaningfully reduces deep sleep - the stage where most physical repair happens.

Sleep impact

1 degree C = less deep sleep

Recovery timeline

20-30% longer in heat

Recovery is training. Protect it as fiercely as you protect your sessions.

Practical steps: train earlier in the day to allow core temperature to drop before sleep. Prioritise electrolyte replacement in the evening. Consider a cool shower before bed. And give yourself permission to take one more rest day per week in July and August.

Train smart.
