The science

How light shapes sleep

Your body clock uses light to decide when to feel sleepy. Bright light in the evening, especially from screens, tells it to wait. Dim, warm light tells it to wind down. In winter we often get too little light by day and too much by night, which flattens the signal that keeps sleep deep and regular.

A good evening has a shape. Eat dinner at a sensible time, finish caffeine by early afternoon, dim the lights and give yourself a buffer between work and bed. If you train in the evening, allow time to cool down afterwards, because a high core temperature and a racing heart make it harder to drop off.

If you wake in the night, resist checking the phone. The bright screen tells your brain it is morning. Keep a notebook by the bed, jot down whatever is on your mind and go back to sleep. Within two weeks most people find their sleep is deeper and their training feels easier.
