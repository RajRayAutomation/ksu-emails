The science

What strength does for endurance

Heavier, lower repetition work improves the force your muscles can produce and the efficiency of the nerves that drive them. For endurance athletes this tends to show up as better economy, meaning less effort at the same pace, plus some protection against injury. Base season is the right time because there is less competition between hard endurance work and heavy lifting for your recovery.

A simple winter strength template: a squat or leg press, a hip hinge such as a deadlift or kettlebell swing, a single leg exercise such as a split squat or step up, a push, a pull and a core move. Three sets of six to ten repetitions, with a weight that makes the last two reps challenging but clean. Thirty minutes, twice a week.

Do not train to failure while you are also building endurance. Leave a couple of reps in the tank, focus on good form and let the work add up over weeks. If you are new to lifting, a session or two with a qualified coach is the best money you will spend this winter.
