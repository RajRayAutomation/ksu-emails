The science

Why minerals matter to athletes

You lose magnesium in sweat, and many people do not get enough from food in the first place. Sodium, potassium and calcium work alongside it to carry the electrical signals that make muscles contract and relax. A balanced mix beats a single mineral on its own, which is why a full electrolyte profile is more useful than salt alone.

A quick audit is worth doing. Think about the last week. Did you wake with cramps? Did your legs feel restless at night? Were you unusually tired midweek? None of these prove a mineral shortfall, and persistent symptoms deserve a conversation with your GP, but they are useful clues. Sweat losses, a busy diet and winter heating can all push minerals lower than you would expect.

The good news is that the fix is usually simple: more mineral rich food, a proper electrolyte drink around training and enough sleep to let your muscles relax. Give it two weeks and notice whether the cramps, restlessness or heavy legs ease. Small changes, steady effect.
