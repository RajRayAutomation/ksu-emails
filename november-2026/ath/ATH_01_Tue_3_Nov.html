The science

Why dark mornings feel harder

Morning light helps set your body clock and nudges alertness upward. When you wake and train in darkness, that signal arrives late, so the first twenty minutes can feel heavy. It is a normal response to less light, not a sign you are losing fitness. Bright light as soon as you wake, then outdoor light as early as you can, helps your body catch up.

The practical side is straightforward. Decide the night before what the session is, how long it will take and what you will wear. Dark mornings punish improvisation. Then get light into your eyes early: open the curtains, switch on bright lights while you get ready and, if you can, spend the first few minutes of the session outside. A short warm up indoors, with some mobility work and a few easy movements, takes the sting out of a cold start.

Be kind to yourself. The first week after the clocks change is the hardest. By the second week your body clock has adjusted and the dark feels less personal. Treat this week as a transition, keep sessions short if you need to and focus on turning up. Consistency in November is the whole game.
