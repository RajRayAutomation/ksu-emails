The science

Why sleep changes in midlife

Progesterone has a calming influence, and as levels fluctuate and fall in perimenopause many women notice lighter, more broken sleep. Night sweats, a busy mind and a warm bedroom do not help. Persistent sleep problems are always worth discussing with your GP. Alongside that, a cool dark room, a regular bedtime and a quiet last hour make a real difference for many women.

Build a short routine you can repeat. A warm drink without caffeine, a bath or shower, ten minutes of stretching or reading, lights low and phone away. The point is to give your brain a clear signal that the day is over. Repetition does the work, so keep it the same each night.

If night sweats are part of the picture, keep the room cool, use breathable bedding and keep a glass of water by the bed. If sleep stays difficult for more than a few weeks, speak to your GP. There are options, and you do not have to struggle on alone.
