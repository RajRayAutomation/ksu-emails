Picture a woman we will call Sue. Fifty four, a lifelong walker who never touched a weight. Her friend dragged her to a beginner class.

Strength is not about the gym. It is about being able to live the life you want.

The science

What strength training does

From midlife, muscle mass tends to decline unless you give it a reason to stay. Resistance exercise helps maintain muscle and bone, supports balance and joint stability and can improve how you feel day to day. You do not need a gym. Two or three short sessions a week with bands, dumbbells or bodyweight is a solid start, and it is never too late to begin.

Start light and focus on form. Learning to move well matters more than lifting heavy in the first month. A qualified trainer or an online beginner programme designed for women in midlife can set you up well. Aim for two or three sessions a week of thirty minutes, with a rest day between.

Do not be discouraged if progress feels slow. Muscle responds to consistency over months. Take photos, note how your clothes fit and how everyday tasks feel. The best results are rarely about the scale. They are about how you feel in your own body.
