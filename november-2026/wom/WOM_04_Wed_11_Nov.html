The science

Why midlife is a bone moment

Oestrogen helps protect bone, so the years around menopause are when bone loss can speed up. From October to March sunlight in the UK is too weak for your skin to make vitamin D, and the NHS advises considering a daily 10 microgram supplement in autumn and winter. Weight bearing and resistance exercise, enough protein and calcium rich foods all play a part.

Strength for bones does not need to be dramatic. Impact and load are what bone responds to, so brisk walking, climbing stairs, dancing and carrying shopping all count, with resistance exercise adding more. If you have been told you have low bone density, ask your GP or a physiotherapist what is safe for you.

Nutrition completes the picture. Calcium rich foods such as dairy, tinned fish with bones, almonds and leafy greens, enough protein, and vitamin D through the winter months. These are everyday actions that quietly protect your future self.
