Why you hit a wall at 3pm, and what to do about it.

The afternoon slump is common in midlife. It is often about blood sugar, sleep and stress rather than willpower.

Picture a woman we will call Diane. By 3pm every day she is reaching for biscuits, scrolling her phone and counting the hours.

She thought she was lazy. She was running on a rollercoaster.

Energy is not a character trait. It is a result of what you eat, how you sleep and how you handle stress.

The science

Blood sugar, sleep and the slump

A breakfast made mostly of refined carbohydrate and a lunch eaten at speed can send blood sugar up and down through the day, and the dip is felt as fatigue and cravings. Poor sleep and stress hormones make it worse. Protein, fibre and healthy fat at each meal, a short walk after lunch and an earlier last coffee flatten the curve for many women.

Look at when you eat, not just what. Leaving five or six hours between a late breakfast and a late lunch is a common cause of the slump. A small protein rich snack, such as yoghurt and nuts or an egg, can bridge the gap. Pair carbohydrates with protein and fibre rather than eating them on their own.

Do not forget water and sleep. Mild dehydration and a poor night both make the afternoon harder. Small adjustments to all three often work better than any single fix.
