The science

Hormones and joints

Oestrogen has effects on connective tissue, inflammation and fluid balance, and many women report new aches, stiffness or sore joints around perimenopause and menopause. Research is ongoing, and other causes are possible, so anything persistent or swollen should be checked by your GP. Gentle movement, healthy weight, omega 3 rich foods and good sleep are all sensible first steps.

Keep a short note of when your aches are worst. Mornings? After sitting? After a poor night? Patterns help you and your GP work out what is going on. Also notice what eases them, whether that is movement, warmth or a day with better sleep.

Foods such as oily fish, olive oil, nuts, berries and leafy greens are part of an eating pattern associated with less inflammation. Alongside gentle strengthening for the muscles around your joints, this gives you a sensible, low risk starting point.
