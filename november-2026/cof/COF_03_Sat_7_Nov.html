Most of the time the beans are fine. The method needs a nudge.

Great coffee is mostly freshness, water and patience.

The science

What changes the cup

Four things matter most. Grind just before brewing, because aroma fades quickly after grinding. Use water just off the boil, roughly 92 to 96 degrees. Weigh your coffee and water rather than guessing. And keep your equipment clean, because old oils taste bitter. Whole bean coffee gives you control over the first of these.

Start with clean water that tastes good. If your tap water is hard or heavily chlorinated, a simple filter makes a surprising difference. Heat it to just below boiling and pour in slow circles over the grounds, giving the coffee time to bloom before you add the rest.

Taste and adjust. Too sour, grind finer or brew longer. Too bitter, grind coarser or shorten the time. Change one thing at a time and write it down. In a week you will have your own perfect recipe.
