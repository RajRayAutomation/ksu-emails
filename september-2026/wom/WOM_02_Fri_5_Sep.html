The science

The estrobolome — your gut's hormonal regulation system

The estrobolome is the collection of gut microbiome genes capable of metabolising oestrogens. Specific gut bacteria produce an enzyme called beta-glucuronidase, which deconjugates oestrogens processed by the liver for excretion. This deconjugated oestrogen is then reabsorbed into circulation rather than eliminated. When the estrobolome is functioning well, oestrogen metabolism is balanced. When gut dysbiosis disrupts it, oestrogen recirculates inappropriately — contributing to oestrogen dominance: heavy periods, bloating, mood instability. Conversely, too little gut bacterial activity clears oestrogen too rapidly, producing low-oestrogen symptoms even when production is adequate. The gut is actively regulating your hormones.
