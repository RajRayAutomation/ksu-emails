They are the backup hormonal system — and for many women, they are the difference between menopause as a manageable transition and menopause as a decade of suffering.

The science

Adrenal health and its role in hormonal resilience after menopause

The adrenal glands produce cortisol, adrenaline, aldosterone and the adrenal androgens including DHEA. In reproductive years, DHEA is a relatively minor contributor to sex hormone levels. Post-menopause, it becomes the primary precursor for both oestrogen and testosterone in peripheral tissue conversion. Adrenal DHEA production peaks in the mid-20s and declines steadily thereafter. By menopause, adrenal DHEA production has already declined by 50 to 70% from its peak. Women who enter menopause with well-supported adrenal function have more DHEA available for peripheral conversion, more hormonal reserve and better outcomes across every menopausal symptom domain.
