Bone density loss is silent. You do not feel it until a fracture tells you it has happened. By which point years of preventable loss have already occurred. The window for meaningful intervention is the decade on either side of menopause.

The science

Oestrogen, progesterone and bone — the full picture

Oestrogen inhibits osteoclast activity — the cells that break down bone tissue. Its decline in menopause removes this inhibition and accelerates bone resorption. What is less widely known is progesterone's role. Progesterone directly stimulates osteoblast activity — the cells that build bone. When progesterone declines in perimenopause, years before significant oestrogen decline, bone building slows while resorption continues at its normal rate. The result is a net loss that begins earlier than most women expect.
