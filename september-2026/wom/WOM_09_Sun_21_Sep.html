A fact that consistently surprises women when they first hear it. Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in postmenopausal women — ahead of breast cancer, ahead of all other causes.

A patient Dr Anna describes: Margaret, 56 years old, focused exclusively on managing her menopausal symptoms. She had never been told that her cardiovascular risk had approximately doubled in the five years since her last period.

Oestrogen is profoundly cardioprotective. It maintains arterial elasticity, supports favourable lipid profiles, reduces inflammatory markers and promotes nitric oxide production — the molecule that keeps blood vessels open and responsive. Its loss in menopause removes a layer of cardiovascular protection that most women did not know they had until it was gone.

The science

The cardiovascular transition of menopause — what changes and why

Before menopause, women have significantly lower rates of cardiovascular disease than men of the same age. This protection is largely oestrogen-mediated. After menopause, the gap closes within a decade. LDL cholesterol rises. HDL cholesterol falls. Triglycerides increase. Blood pressure tends to rise. Arterial stiffness increases. These are the direct cardiovascular consequences of oestrogen withdrawal.
