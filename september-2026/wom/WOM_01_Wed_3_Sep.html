The science

What is actually happening in perimenopause and why September matters

Perimenopause typically begins between 40 and 50. The defining hormonal event is not the drop in oestrogen — it is the decline in progesterone. Progesterone falls by up to 75% between the ages of 35 and 50. It governs sleep quality, mood regulation, anxiety response, bone density maintenance and vaginal tissue integrity. Its decline precedes oestrogen decline by years. September accelerates this. As daylight decreases, cortisol rises to compensate for falling serotonin. The cortisol surge competes with progesterone for precursor molecules in the pregnenolone steal — and progesterone always loses.
