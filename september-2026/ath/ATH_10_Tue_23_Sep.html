The science

What fat adaptation actually does and does not do

Fat adaptation increases the proportion of fat used as fuel at a given intensity, preserving glycogen for high-intensity efforts. What it does not do is eliminate the need for carbohydrate at intensities above approximately 75% of VO2 max. At higher intensities, the glycolytic pathway is simply faster than beta-oxidation can match. Elite athletes who are fully fat-adapted still consume carbohydrates during high-intensity racing because the physics of energy production at maximal effort require it. Exogenous ketones offer a middle path — providing the clean alternative fuel benefits of ketosis without requiring the dietary restriction that can compromise high-intensity capacity.
