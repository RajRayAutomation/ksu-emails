The science

Growth hormone, slow-wave sleep and why your sessions need the night

Approximately 70% of your daily growth hormone release occurs during slow-wave sleep, specifically in the first two hours after sleep onset. Growth hormone drives muscle protein synthesis — the process by which your muscles repair and grow stronger in response to training. Without adequate slow-wave sleep, training creates the damage without completing the repair. Cortisol, which rises when sleep quality is poor, actively opposes growth hormone and breaks down muscle tissue. The athlete who trains hard and sleeps poorly is simultaneously building and destroying.

The electrolyte connection is direct. Magnesium — one of the four minerals in Real Ketones Orange Elevate — is a co-factor in melatonin production and GABA activity, both of which promote deeper sleep architecture. Athletes who are magnesium deficient, which is most of them, typically show reduced slow-wave sleep duration.

Fun fact: A study in the Journal of Research in Medical Sciences found that magnesium supplementation significantly increased slow-wave sleep duration and reduced cortisol levels in participants with low magnesium status.
