The science

The dehydration performance curve — what the research actually shows

A 2% reduction in body water reduces aerobic performance by approximately 10 to 20%. Reaction time slows. Decision-making degrades. Perceived exertion increases — the same effort feels harder. In a twice-daily training block in September, when sweat rates are still elevated from summer heat, Elena was arriving at her second session already behind.

The solution is not simply drinking more water. It is replacing what sweat actually removes. Sodium, potassium, magnesium, calcium. Water without electrolytes does not rehydrate you — it dilutes what remains. Elena now takes Real Ketones Orange Elevate before both sessions. Her energy consistency across the training day has transformed.

Fun fact: Research from the American College of Sports Medicine recommends athletes begin exercise in a euhydrated state and consume electrolyte-containing fluids rather than plain water during sessions longer than 60 minutes. Electrolytes prevent hyponatraemia — dangerously low sodium from over-drinking plain water — as much as they prevent dehydration.
