The science

The circadian rhythm and why morning light is medicine

Your circadian rhythm is regulated primarily by light exposure to specialised cells in your retina called intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells. These cells contain melanopsin, sensitive to blue-spectrum light. In natural morning light, this exposure signals the brain's master clock to initiate the cortisol awakening response, boost serotonin production and suppress melatonin. This cascade sets the timing of every hormone release, metabolic process and sleep-wake transition for the next 24 hours.
