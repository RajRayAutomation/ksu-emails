The science

Acetylcholine and the attention system

Acetylcholine is synthesised from choline — a nutrient that cannot be made in sufficient quantities by the body and must be obtained from diet or supplementation. The primary dietary sources are eggs, liver and fish. People who eat few of these are consistently choline deficient. In the context of cognitive function, acetylcholine governs the ability to direct and sustain attention, to encode new memories and to maintain working memory during complex tasks. Alpha GPC — alpha-glycerylphosphorylcholine — is the most bioavailable form of choline available as a supplement. At 300mg per serving it raises plasma and brain choline levels within 30 to 60 minutes of ingestion.

Alex added Keto Brainz to his morning coffee. Within three weeks he reported that his sustained attention window had extended to three hours consistently. Within six weeks he described it as feeling like the version of himself from five years ago.

Fun fact: Eggs are the richest dietary source of choline, with approximately 147mg per large egg. To meet the adequate intake for adults — 550mg per day for men, 425mg for women — you would need to eat three to four eggs daily. Most people eat significantly fewer. Supplemental Alpha GPC fills the gap directly.
