The Aponte region sits in the Andes of Colombia at over 1,800 metres above sea level. The Inga people have farmed this land for centuries. At this altitude the coffee cherries develop slowly, over a longer growing season, in cooler temperatures and volcanic soil rich in minerals.

The result is a bean with a complexity and a density of flavour compounds that lower-altitude growing simply cannot replicate. When you slow the ripening process, you increase the concentration of the precursor molecules that become the flavour, aroma and antioxidant compounds in the final cup.

The science

The chemistry of altitude-grown coffee

Coffee beans contain hundreds of volatile aromatic compounds, chlorogenic acids and other polyphenols that develop during the ripening of the coffee cherry. At high altitude, the slower ripening process allows more complete development of these compounds. Chlorogenic acids — the primary antioxidant compounds in coffee — are present in significantly higher concentrations in high-altitude beans and are partially degraded by excessive heat during rapid lowland ripening.

Sacred Cups works directly with the Inga farming community on fair trade terms that are not a certification label but the foundation of the entire business model. When you buy Aponte Inga you are buying into a supply chain that is transparent, ethical and built to sustain the community that produces it.

Fun fact: Chlorogenic acids in coffee have been shown in multiple studies to support healthy blood glucose metabolism, liver function and systemic antioxidant capacity. The concentration varies significantly by growing altitude, processing method and roast level.
