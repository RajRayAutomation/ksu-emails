Women in perimenopause are measurably more susceptible to upper respiratory infections in autumn than both premenopausal women and postmenopausal women on stable hormone therapy. This is not anecdote. It is immunology.

A patient called Faye. 46 years old. She had been reliably ill for two to three weeks every October for the past four years. Previously she had sailed through autumn without significant illness. The timing correlated precisely with the onset of perimenopausal hormonal fluctuation.

The science

How oestrogen regulates immune function and what happens when it fluctuates

Oestrogen receptors are present on virtually every immune cell type, including natural killer cells, T cells, B cells and macrophages. Oestrogen enhances innate immune responses, supports mucosal immunity in the respiratory tract, and modulates the inflammatory response to viral infection. In perimenopause, oestrogen does not decline in a smooth linear fashion. It fluctuates wildly, sometimes higher than premenopausal levels, sometimes dramatically lower, often varying by 50% within a single cycle. This fluctuation, not the eventual decline, is what disrupts immune regulation. The immune system receives inconsistent hormonal signalling and responds with inconsistent function.
