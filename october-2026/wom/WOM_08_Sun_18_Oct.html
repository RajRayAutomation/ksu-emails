Testosterone, DHEA and the autumn libido shift

Female libido is governed primarily by testosterone and DHEA, with oestrogen playing a supporting role in genital sensitivity and arousal response. Both testosterone and DHEA show measurable seasonal variation, with the lowest levels occurring in autumn and winter, likely as an evolutionary adaptation to reduced reproductive advantage in winter months. In premenopausal women, the seasonal drop is buffered by overall hormonal reserve. In perimenopausal women, who already have reduced adrenal DHEA production and fluctuating testosterone, the autumn seasonal drop falls below the threshold for adequate libido maintenance in many cases.
