The most important update in women's hormonal health in a decade happened without most women being told about it.

In 2024, the British Menopause Society updated its guidance on hormone therapy significantly. The revision clarified that for women under 60, or within ten years of menopause onset, the benefits of hormone therapy substantially outweigh the risks for the majority of women. The breast cancer risk that dominated the conversation for twenty years following the 2002 WHI study was found to be attributable primarily to synthetic progestin, not to bioidentical progesterone.

A patient called Helen. 53 years old. She had refused to consider hormone therapy for seven years based on her understanding of the breast cancer risk from a newspaper article she had read in 2003. The conversation we had in October 2025 was the first time she had been given the updated picture.

The science

What the 2024 guidance revision actually shows

The 2024 BMS guidance revision confirmed that transdermal oestrogen, applied as a patch or gel rather than taken orally, does not increase clotting risk and is safe for women with a history of migraines. Micronised progesterone, the bioidentical form, does not carry the breast cancer association found in earlier studies using synthetic progestins. The updated guidance explicitly states that for most women under 60, the cardiovascular protection, bone density preservation, cognitive support and quality of life benefits of hormone therapy outweigh the risks when the right type is used at the right time.

Fun fact: The timing hypothesis in hormone therapy research shows that women who begin HRT within ten years of menopause onset show cardiovascular benefit, while women who begin more than ten years after show neutral or slightly elevated cardiovascular risk. The window of benefit is open in perimenopause. It closes with delay.
