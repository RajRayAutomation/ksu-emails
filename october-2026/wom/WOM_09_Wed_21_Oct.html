The science

Oestrogen, seasonal variation and cognitive performance in perimenopausal women

The hippocampus, the brain region central to working memory and verbal recall, has oestrogen receptors throughout its structure. Oestrogen supports synaptic plasticity, cerebral blood flow and the production of BDNF in hippocampal tissue. In perimenopause, oestrogen fluctuates rather than declining smoothly. The autumn fluctuation, driven by the interaction between the seasonal HPA axis shift and the already-unstable HPO axis, can produce the most dramatic oestrogen drops of the entire perimenopausal transition. For women like Sara, whose cognitive performance is tightly coupled to oestrogen-supported hippocampal function, these drops produce the cognitive symptoms she described.

Fun fact: A 2023 study in Neurology found that perimenopausal women showed greater variability in cognitive test performance month to month than either premenopausal or postmenopausal women, with the lowest scores correlating with the lowest oestrogen measurements. The cognitive variability of perimenopause is real, measurable and hormonally driven.
