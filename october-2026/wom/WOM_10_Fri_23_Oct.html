A statistic worth knowing.

Cardiovascular events, including heart attacks and strokes, are more frequent in autumn and winter than in spring and summer. The increase is not small. UK data shows approximately 30% more cardiovascular events in October through January than in June through September. The seasonal pattern is not random. It is physiological.

A patient called Gillian. 55 years old. She had been managing borderline blood pressure for two years. Every October her readings rose noticeably above their summer baseline. Her GP adjusted her medication twice in October over two years without ever identifying the pattern.

The science

Why October raises cardiovascular risk in perimenopausal women

Four mechanisms converge in October for perimenopausal women. Elevated cortisol increases blood pressure directly through vasoconstriction and sodium retention. Reduced oestrogen removes its cardioprotective effect on arterial elasticity and nitric oxide production. Cold temperature causes peripheral vasoconstriction, raising peripheral vascular resistance and therefore blood pressure. And seasonal increases in inflammatory markers, including CRP and interleukin-6, promote endothelial dysfunction. In premenopausal women, oestrogen buffers most of these effects. In perimenopausal women, the buffer is incomplete and unreliable.

In perimenopausal women, this seasonal increase is approximately double the population average due to the loss of oestrogen-mediated vascular protection.
