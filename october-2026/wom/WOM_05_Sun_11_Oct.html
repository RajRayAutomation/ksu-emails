Why slow-wave sleep contracts in autumn for perimenopausal women

Slow-wave sleep is regulated by two overlapping systems: the homeostatic sleep drive, which builds through the waking day, and the circadian influence of core body temperature drop. Progesterone plays a critical role in both, through its GABAergic sedative effect and its influence on core temperature regulation. As progesterone declines in perimenopause and autumn cortisol further disrupts the hormonal balance, the slow-wave sleep trigger becomes less reliable. The result is a shift toward lighter, more fragmented sleep that feels like insomnia even when total sleep time is adequate.
