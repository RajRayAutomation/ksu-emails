The science

The October hormonal cascade in perimenopause

As daylight decreases in October, the pineal gland shifts melatonin production earlier. The hypothalamus responds by signalling the adrenal axis to compensate for falling light-driven serotonin with increased cortisol. For women in perimenopause, whose progesterone is already declining, this cortisol surge triggers the pregnenolone steal, depleting the already-limited progesterone precursor supply. The result is a compound hormonal drop that arrives every October with seasonal reliability. It is not mood. It is not weakness. It is biochemistry on a calendar.
