The research on habit formation is consistent on one point that most people miss. Habits formed during periods of environmental change are more robust than habits formed during stable periods. The brain is more plastic, more receptive to encoding new routines, during transitions.

October is the biggest environmental transition of the year in the UK. Light, temperature, schedule, social patterns all shift simultaneously. A habit built into the October morning is building itself into a neural groove at the moment when the groove-making is easiest.

The science

Why transitions create neurological opportunity

The basal ganglia, the brain structure responsible for habit encoding, shows increased synaptic plasticity during periods of environmental novelty. New stimuli, new schedules and new sensory environments promote BDNF production in habit-related circuits, making new behaviours more likely to consolidate into automatic routines. October's environmental novelty, cold air, changed light, new routines, creates precisely this condition. A behaviour repeated consistently in the first two weeks of October, during the peak transition period, encodes more deeply than the same behaviour repeated in stable mid-summer conditions.

This is why October is the month to lock in the Keto Brainz morning protocol rather than treat it as optional. The neurological conditions for habit formation are as good as they get. The cortisol awakening response is shifting. The morning feels different. That difference is the plasticity window. Use it.

Fun fact: A 2022 study in Nature Human Behaviour found that habit formation rates were significantly higher in participants who began new behaviours during seasonal transitions than those who began the same behaviours during stable seasonal periods. January resolutions fail partly because January, despite feeling like a transition, has consistent cold and dark that the brain has already adapted to.
