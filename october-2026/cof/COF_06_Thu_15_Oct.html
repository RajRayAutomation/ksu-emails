Adenosine, melatonin and the October evening caffeine problem

Caffeine works by blocking adenosine receptors. Adenosine is the sleep-pressure molecule that builds through the waking day and promotes deep sleep when it reaches threshold levels. Blocking adenosine receptors with caffeine delays this threshold. In summer, when melatonin onset is later, a 4pm coffee resolves before it conflicts with the natural sleep-drive peak. In October, when melatonin begins rising earlier, the same 4pm coffee is still blocking adenosine receptors when the melatonin signal arrives. The two systems conflict and the result is reduced slow-wave sleep, which is exactly the restorative sleep October demands most.
