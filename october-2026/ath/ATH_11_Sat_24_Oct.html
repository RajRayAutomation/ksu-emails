The science

The clock change and athletic performance

The cortisol awakening response, the hormonal surge that primes your body for physical and cognitive performance, is anchored to your biological clock rather than the clock on the wall. When the clocks change, the cortisol peak arrives at what the clock now calls 7am rather than 8am. Athletes who train at a fixed clock time find themselves training before the cortisol peak for three to five days until the circadian rhythm adjusts. The result is sessions that feel flat, times that are slightly slower and recovery that is slightly less efficient. The solution is to train 30 minutes later than usual for the first week after the change.
