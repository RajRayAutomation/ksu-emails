The science

Open window theory and the October immune dip

During intense exercise, cortisol and adrenaline temporarily suppress natural killer cell activity and mucosal immunoglobulin A secretion. This creates a window of approximately 90 minutes post-session during which the athlete is measurably more susceptible to upper respiratory infection. In October, this window coincides with seasonal rhinovirus peaks and the return to indoor environments after summer. The combination is what produces the October illness pattern. Beta-hydroxybutyrate, the primary ketone in Real Ketones Orange Elevate, has demonstrated direct anti-inflammatory and immune-modulating properties. Athletes who supplement consistently show attenuated post-exercise cortisol spikes, which narrows the open window.

Her October illness did not materialise this year.

Fun fact: Mucosal immunoglobulin A, the primary immune defence of the upper respiratory tract, drops by up to 50% in the 90 minutes after a high-intensity training session. Adequate recovery nutrition, particularly carbohydrate and electrolyte repletion, restores it faster. Ketones support this restoration through their anti-inflammatory mechanism.
