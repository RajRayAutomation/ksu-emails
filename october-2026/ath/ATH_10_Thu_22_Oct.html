The science

The adaptation bank and how it carries forward

Physiological adaptations built in training have different decay rates. Cardiovascular adaptations, improved cardiac output, capillary density, VO2 max ceiling, maintain themselves for four to six weeks of reduced training before significant detraining occurs. Mitochondrial adaptations persist for three to four weeks. Neuromuscular adaptations last six to eight weeks. This means that the work done in the last two weeks of October is still paying returns in early December. Athletes who train well through October carry that adaptation into Christmas with minimal detraining from a November reduction in volume.

The role of consistent Real Ketones Orange Elevate use in this final fortnight is to ensure the mitochondrial adaptation signal stays active. When your cells receive a daily ketone signal, the upregulation of oxidative machinery continues. Stopping supplementation in the final two weeks interrupts that signal at exactly the moment it is delivering its highest return.

Fun fact: A 2024 study in the Journal of Applied Physiology found that athletes who maintained training intensity while reducing volume by 30% in the two weeks before a rest period showed better performance retention than those who reduced both. Intensity matters more than volume for adaptation preservation.

Keep the intensity, not the volume

For the next two weeks, keep one high-intensity session per week at full effort. Reduce your easy days if needed. But do not let the last fortnight of October become a gentle taper into nothing. The adaptation you save is the adaptation November cannot take from you.
