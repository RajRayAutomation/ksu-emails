October is the month when training load climbs and fuelling strategy gets complicated.

A duathlete called Claire. 35 years old. October was always her highest mileage month. She trained well but ate the same across the year regardless of load. By week three of October she was consistently running out of fuel in the back half of her long sessions.

Claire's problem was not effort. It was that she had not adjusted her fuelling to match the volume she was now asking her body to handle. The same intake that maintained her in August was creating a deficit in October.

The science

Carbohydrate periodisation and the ketone bridge

Carbohydrate periodisation, deliberately varying carbohydrate intake to match training load, is one of the most evidence-supported nutrition strategies in endurance sport. On high-load days, carbohydrate supports glycolytic performance. On recovery days, lower carbohydrate and consistent ketone supplementation trains fat oxidation and allows metabolic flexibility to develop. The mistake most athletes make is applying summer fuelling to autumn training loads. October volume requires both higher total carbohydrate and strategic ketone supplementation to manage the recovery debt that accumulates between sessions.

Claire began periodising deliberately. High carbohydrate on interval and long session days. Real Ketones Orange Elevate on recovery days and as a pre-session primer on all days. Her back-half energy in long sessions transformed within a fortnight. The fuel was matching the demand.

Fun fact: Research from the Australian Institute of Sport found that athletes who periodise carbohydrate intake around training load rather than maintaining consistent intake show better fat oxidation rates at submaximal effort and equivalent performance at maximal effort after twelve weeks.
