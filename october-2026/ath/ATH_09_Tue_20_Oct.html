The science

What cold does to race-day physiology

In cold conditions, peripheral blood flow is reduced as the body prioritises core temperature maintenance. This means the muscles of the extremities receive slightly less oxygen in the opening miles of a race than they would in warmer conditions, until core temperature rises sufficiently to allow peripheral vasodilation. Athletes who start too fast in cold conditions hit this physiological barrier early. The optimal strategy is a slightly more conservative first quarter and a fuel source that supports thermogenesis from the outset. Exogenous ketones taken 30 minutes before the start support both energy provision and the thermogenic process, meaning the peripheral vasodilation happens faster.
